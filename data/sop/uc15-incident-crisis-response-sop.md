# SOP-OPS-003: Network incident and crisis response coordination

> **Fictitious document.** Qanat Transport and Mobility Agency, its network, severity levels, contact numbers, systems and staff titles are invented for demonstration. Nothing here describes a real authority, real law or real emergency arrangements.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Document id | SOP-OPS-003 |
| Version | 1.0 (synthetic) |
| Owner | Head of Operations Control, Qanat Transport and Mobility Agency |
| Related use case | UC15: Incident and crisis response coordination |
| Applies to | Incidents that disrupt Qanat's bus, metro feeder and road operations: detection, classification, notification, command, response actions, passenger information, stand-down and review |
| Review cycle | Every 12 months, and after every Level 3 incident |

## 1. Purpose

Detect incidents early, classify them consistently, get the right people acting quickly, keep passengers and the public correctly informed, and restore service safely. Control room tools may correlate alerts, suggest the playbook response and draft messages; a named Duty Officer commands every incident. Actions that cannot be undone once executed (suspending a line, evacuating a station, requesting a road closure) and every public message need the Duty Officer's or, in a crisis, the Head of Operations Control's approval. Emergency services lead on anything involving life safety.

## 2. Scope

**In scope:** operational incidents on Qanat's network (breakdowns blocking a route, collisions, infrastructure failures, power and systems outages, severe weather, crowding, security alerts); severity classification; notification; command; operational response; passenger information; stand-down; post-incident review.

**Out of scope:** the emergency services' own command of a life-safety incident; accident investigation (SOP-SAF-003); insurance and claims; planned engineering works (SOP-OPS-007); media interviews (Qanat Communications Office).

## 3. Roles

Four job profiles carry out this procedure. Each has a distinct purpose, a fixed set of tasks and its own limits.

### 3.1 Control Room Operator
- **Purpose:** detect, log and classify incidents, notify the right people and carry out approved actions.
- **Works with:** the Duty Officer, depot controllers, field supervisors, emergency services.
- **Authority:** may open and update the incident log, classify by the severity table, send notifications, call emergency services for any danger to life, propose playbook actions and carry out approved actions. May not approve a service suspension, evacuation or road closure request, or publish a public message.
- **Skills:** control room systems, the severity table (section 6.1), radio procedure, calm communication.

### 3.2 Operations Control Duty Officer (Incident Commander)
- **Purpose:** command Level 1 and Level 2 incidents and support the crisis lead at Level 3.
- **Works with:** the Operator, the Passenger Information Officer, emergency services, the Head of Operations Control.
- **Authority:** may confirm or change the severity level, approve operational actions including suspension, evacuation and road closure requests at Levels 1 and 2, approve public messages, and stand an incident down.
- **Skills:** incident command, the playbooks, risk judgement under time pressure.

### 3.3 Passenger Information Officer
- **Purpose:** keep passengers and the public correctly informed.
- **Works with:** the Duty Officer, the Communications Office.
- **Authority:** may draft messages from approved templates and publish messages the Duty Officer has approved. May not publish without approval or speak to the media.
- **Skills:** clear public writing in Arabic and English, the message templates (section 6.4), channel tools.

### 3.4 Head of Operations Control (Crisis Lead)
- **Purpose:** lead Level 3 crises and own the procedure.
- **Works with:** the crisis team, Qanat leadership, emergency services, other authorities.
- **Authority:** activates the crisis team; approves actions and public messages at Level 3; approves changes to playbooks and the severity table.
- **Skills:** crisis management, inter-agency coordination.

## 4. Procedure

Task ids are used in the audit log and the training material.

### Step 1. Detect and classify (Control Room Operator)

| Task | Action |
|---|---|
| T01 | Receive the alert (staff radio, CCTV, vehicle telemetry, emergency services, public report) and open an incident in the Incident Log with the time, place and source. If anyone is in danger, call emergency services first. |
| T02 | Classify the severity by the severity table. When unsure between two levels, take the higher. |
| T03 | Link further alerts about the same event to the open incident rather than opening a new one. |

### Step 2. Notify and command

| Task | Action | Role |
|---|---|---|
| T04 | Notify the people in the notification matrix for that level within the target time. | Control Room Operator |
| T05 | Take command, confirm or change the level, and record it. | Duty Officer |
| T06 | For Level 3, activate the crisis team and take the lead. | Head of Operations Control |

### Step 3. Respond

| Task | Action | Role |
|---|---|---|
| T07 | Propose the playbook response for the incident type (diversion, replacement buses, extra staff, holding services). | Control Room Operator |
| T08 | Approve, change or refuse each proposed action. Suspension of a line, evacuation of a station and a road closure request are approved only by the incident commander. | Duty Officer (Level 1–2) or Head of Operations Control (Level 3) |
| T09 | Carry out approved actions and record each with its time and result. | Control Room Operator |

### Step 4. Inform the public

| Task | Action | Role |
|---|---|---|
| T10 | Draft passenger messages from templates using only facts confirmed in the Incident Log. | Passenger Information Officer |
| T11 | Approve or correct each message. | Duty Officer (Level 1–2) or Head of Operations Control (Level 3) |
| T12 | Publish approved messages on the app, stops and stations, and social channels; update at least every 30 minutes until stand-down. | Passenger Information Officer |

### Step 5. Recover and stand down

| Task | Action | Role |
|---|---|---|
| T13 | Confirm normal service is restored and safe, and stand the incident down. | Duty Officer |
| T14 | Publish the all-clear message and close the Incident Log. | Passenger Information Officer and Operator |

### Step 6. Review

| Task | Action | Role |
|---|---|---|
| T15 | Hold a post-incident review for every Level 2 and Level 3 incident and track the actions. | Duty Officer (Level 2), Head of Operations Control (Level 3) |
| T16 | Each month, sample 10% of Level 1 logs against this SOP and run one exercise of a Level 3 playbook each quarter. | Head of Operations Control |

## 5. Timings

| Item | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incident logged | 5 minutes | 2 minutes | 2 minutes |
| Duty Officer in command | 15 minutes | 5 minutes | 5 minutes |
| First public message | 15 minutes | 10 minutes | 10 minutes |
| Message updates | On change | Every 30 minutes | Every 30 minutes |
| Post-incident review | Not required | Within 5 working days | Within 10 working days |

## 6. Severity, notification, playbooks and templates (fictitious)

### 6.1 Severity table

| Level | Examples |
|---|---|
| Level 1 Minor | A delay under 30 minutes on one route; a single vehicle breakdown off the main road; a minor systems fault with a workaround |
| Level 2 Major | A delay over 30 minutes or affecting 3 or more routes; a line or corridor blocked; any injury; a station or depot systems outage; crowding needing control |
| Level 3 Crisis | A fatality or multiple casualties; a network-wide outage; a security threat; severe weather affecting the whole network; an incident needing another authority to lead |

### 6.2 Notification matrix

| Level | Notify |
|---|---|
| Level 1 | Duty Officer; affected depot controller |
| Level 2 | Duty Officer; depot controllers; Passenger Information Officer; Head of Operations Control (inform) |
| Level 3 | All of Level 2; Head of Operations Control (to lead); Chief Operating Officer; Communications Office; emergency services liaison |

### 6.3 Playbooks
PB-01 Vehicle breakdown on route; PB-02 Collision; PB-03 Road blocked; PB-04 Power or systems outage; PB-05 Severe weather; PB-06 Security alert; PB-07 Crowding. Each lists proposed actions, who approves each, and the message templates to use.

### 6.4 Message templates
Every message states what is happening, where, what passengers should do, and when the next update will come. It gives no cause that has not been confirmed and never names or describes injured people.

## 7. Controls

1. Danger to life is reported to emergency services first, before any other step.
2. Every incident has one named commander at any time, recorded in the log.
3. When unsure between levels, the higher level applies until the commander decides.
4. Suspension, evacuation and road closure requests are approved only by the incident commander; once carried out they cannot be undone, only followed by a new action.
5. No public message is published without the commander's approval; messages use only confirmed facts.
6. Every alert, decision, action and message is time-stamped in the Incident Log.
7. Personal data of people involved is kept out of public messages and shared only with emergency services and investigators.

## 8. Exceptions and escalation

| Situation | Handled by | Escalate to |
|---|---|---|
| Danger to life | Operator | Emergency services, at once; Duty Officer |
| Level rises during the incident | Duty Officer | Head of Operations Control at Level 3 |
| Duty Officer unreachable within the target | Operator | Head of Operations Control |
| Emergency services take command | Duty Officer | Supports their commander; Qanat actions continue under their direction |
| Media enquiry | Passenger Information Officer | Communications Office |
| Incident caused by a suspected crime | Duty Officer | Police; SOP-SAF-003 |

## 9. Records

Incident Log (alerts, classification, commander, notifications, decisions, actions, messages), recordings of control room radio, post-incident reviews and action trackers, monthly samples, exercise reports. Kept for 7 years; Level 3 records for 15 years (synthetic retention periods).

## 10. Required records and how they are made

### D1. Incident Log entry
- **What must be true:** time, place, source, level, commander, and each later decision and action with its time.

### D2. Action approval
- **What must be true:** recorded in the log with the commander's name, the time, and the action approved.

### D3. Public message record
- **What must be true:** the approved text, the approver, the channels and the publication time.

### D4. Post-incident review
- **What must be true:** timeline, what worked, what did not, actions with owners and dates.

### SUB. How records are made
All records are made in the Incident Log in real time. Radio decisions are entered by the Operator as they happen and confirmed by the commander at stand-down.

### VAL. When a record stays valid
The level and commander recorded stay in force until changed in the log.

## 11. Detailed description of each task

### T01. Receive and log
- **Job profile:** Control Room Operator.
- **Output:** an open incident with time, place and source; emergency services called if anyone is in danger.

### T02. Classify
- **Job profile:** Control Room Operator.
- **Steps:** apply section 6.1; take the higher level if unsure.
- **Output:** a recorded level.

### T03. Correlate alerts
- **Job profile:** Control Room Operator.
- **Steps:** link alerts with the same place and event window to the open incident; open a new one only for a separate event.
- **Output:** one incident per event.

### T04. Notify
- **Job profile:** Control Room Operator.
- **Output:** notifications sent per section 6.2, logged with times.

### T05. Take command
- **Job profile:** Operations Control Duty Officer.
- **Output:** commander and confirmed level recorded.

### T06. Activate the crisis team
- **Job profile:** Head of Operations Control.
- **Output:** crisis team activated and lead recorded.

### T07. Propose the response
- **Job profile:** Control Room Operator.
- **Steps:** select the playbook; list its actions adapted to the incident; mark which need commander approval.
- **Output:** a proposed action list.

### T08. Approve actions
- **Job profile:** Incident commander.
- **Steps:** approve, change or refuse each action; for suspension, evacuation or road closure, confirm the reason and the alternative for passengers.
- **Output:** D2 for each approved action.

### T09. Carry out and record actions
- **Job profile:** Control Room Operator.
- **Output:** each action done and logged with its result.

### T10. Draft messages
- **Job profile:** Passenger Information Officer.
- **Steps:** use the template; include only facts confirmed in the log; Arabic and English.
- **Output:** a draft message.

### T11. Approve messages
- **Job profile:** Incident commander.
- **Output:** an approved or corrected message.

### T12. Publish and update
- **Job profile:** Passenger Information Officer.
- **Output:** D3 for each message; updates at least every 30 minutes at Levels 2 and 3.

### T13. Stand down
- **Job profile:** Operations Control Duty Officer.
- **Steps:** confirm with field supervisors that the route is clear and safe; reverse temporary actions by new approved actions; stand down.
- **Output:** stand-down recorded.

### T14. All-clear and close
- **Job profile:** Passenger Information Officer and Control Room Operator.
- **Output:** all-clear published and the log closed.

### T15. Post-incident review
- **Job profile:** Duty Officer (Level 2), Head of Operations Control (Level 3).
- **Output:** D4 with tracked actions.

### T16. Sample and exercise
- **Job profile:** Head of Operations Control.
- **Output:** a monthly sample report and a quarterly exercise report.

## 12. Decision rules

### R1. Who decides
The incident commander (Duty Officer at Levels 1 and 2, Head of Operations Control at Level 3) decides every action and message. Emergency services decide life-safety matters. Tools propose; they never decide.

### R2. Life first
Danger to life is reported to emergency services before anything else.

### R3. Level
Classify by section 6.1; if unsure, the higher level.

### R4. Irreversible actions
Suspension, evacuation and road closure requests need the commander's recorded approval.

### R5. Public messages
Approved by the commander; only confirmed facts; no names or descriptions of injured people.

### R6. Stand-down
Only the commander stands an incident down, after confirmation that service is safe.

### R7. Statuses
"Open", "Commanded", "Responding", "Recovering", "Stood down", "Closed".

## 13. Questions staff ask, and the answer under this SOP

### Q1. A bus has hit a pedestrian. What do I do first?
Call emergency services, then log the incident. It is at least Level 2.

### Q2. Can the Operator suspend the line to save time?
No. Only the incident commander approves a suspension (R4).

### Q3. Social media says the crash was caused by the driver. Can we say that?
No. Messages use only confirmed facts (R5).

### Q4. Is this Level 1 or 2? There is a 25-minute delay on four routes.
Level 2: it affects 3 or more routes.
